abstract: |
  **Background:** Lead exposure is a public health concern, posing a severe threat to developing children even at low levels of toxicity. Laser-based analysis of deciduous teeth enables retrospective weekly exposure assessment, revealing historical exposure patterns linkable to lead-induced conditions later in life. However, current regulatory standards for lead exposure focus primarily on blood concentration, including cord blood levels.

  **Objective:** To predict cord blood lead concentrations at birth from weekly child dentine lead levels.

  **Methods:** We measured lead levels in dentine and cord blood of 258 children of the Programming Research in Obesity, Growth, Environment and Social Stress (PROGRESS) cohort. We predicted cord blood lead concentrations from the training dataset (80% of the data) employing several predictive algorithms, including XGBoost and the ensemble-algorithm Super Learner, and assessed their predictive performance in the validation dataset (20% of the data) using the Mean Squared Error (MSE) and the area under the Receiver-operating characteristic curve (AUC). We iterated the training-validation procedure 100 times to minimize overfitting.

  **Results:** Children had moderate cord blood lead concentrations (mean: 33.5 µg/L; SD: 25.6). XGBoost was selected as final predictive model (MSE: 0.55; AUC: 0.842). Predicted lead concentrations were significantly correlated with actual values (r=0.66). The most relevant timepoints for predicting cord blood lead concentration were 2-weeks before and after birth. Children with cord blood lead concentrations >35 µg/L were identified with 73% specificity and 80% sensitivity.

  **Conclusions:** We assessed perinatal lead exposure from dentine lead levels offering a novel retrospective tool for identifying children at-risk of developing lead-induced conditions.
